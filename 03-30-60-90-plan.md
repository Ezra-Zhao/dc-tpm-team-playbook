# 30-60-90 Build-Out Plan - Founding TPM Manager (Design)

> **DESIGN ARTIFACT.** This is the plan the candidate would execute if hired
> as the founding manager of the DC Infrastructure TPM function. It is a
> plan, not a history: he has not built a TPM team, and this document is the
> substitute evidence - judge it as a plan. The order below is deliberate and
> is the first thing to defend in an interview: **visibility first, then
> people, then coverage** - because hiring into an invisible program scales
> confusion, and expanding coverage before the records discipline holds just
> multiplies reporting debt.

## Days 0-30 - Make the current portfolio legible (visibility)

Goal: by day 30, leadership can see every active site's baseline vs
forecast, top risks, and open decisions in one place - produced from the
team's own records, not assembled for the occasion.

- Inventory every active site and program: governing milestones, current
  baseline of record (or the honest finding that none exists), top risks,
  open decisions and their ages.
- Stand up the three records (status / risk / decision, per
  02-program-review-pack.md) for the portfolio as it actually is - including
  the gaps. The first register will be ugly; an ugly true register beats a
  clean invented one.
- Run the first weekly site reviews and the first portfolio review on the
  new cadence, personally, to learn where the signal breaks.
- Meet every interface owner in the charter (Construction, Compute,
  Security and Compliance, Energy/Location, Operations, OFE delivery) and
  agree the two boundary rules that matter most: turnover's dual
  accountability, and the OFE/site-schedule date boundary.
- Learn the sites physically: walk at least the nearest-term site end to
  end. A TPM lead who has not stood in the white space is managing
  documents.
- Deliverable at day 30: first exec one-pager per program, from records;
  a written "state of delivery visibility" note - what is known, what is
  not, and what it would take to know it.

Explicitly not in the first 30 days: reorganizing anyone's reporting line,
launching a tooling migration, or promising dates to leadership. All three
are ways to spend trust before earning it.

## Days 31-60 - Staff the sharp end (people)

Goal: hiring is open and calibrated for the roles that protect the nearest
turnover dates; the function's standards exist in writing before the team
that must hold them arrives.

- Finalize the scorecard and loop (01-hiring-scorecard-and-interview-loop.md)
  with the hiring partners; run the first calibrations on live candidates.
- Open requisitions in this order: (1) Site TPM for the site with the
  nearest turnover, (2) Portfolio TPM to own cross-site dependencies and
  the records discipline, (3) further Site TPMs only as site count demands.
- Begin structured onboarding design from the candidate's own real mentoring
  experience in operations: paired site walks, first-review shadowing,
  verification-discipline coaching on live items. (Onboarding new hires is
  something he actually does today; that experience is cited as fact, the
  TPM-specific content is design.)
- Shadow-then-delegate: the lead keeps running portfolio review while new
  TPMs take site reviews, with the lead reviewing their records before the
  room sees them - calibration through artifacts, not through hovering.
- Deliverable at day 60: at least the first requisition through a full
  calibrated loop; onboarding pack v1; the lead's own site-review load
  measurably reduced because records, not the lead, carry the status.

## Days 61-90 - Extend coverage without diluting the bar (coverage)

Goal: the cadence and records now run across all active sites with the
team - not the lead - producing them, and the function's first
multi-quarter view exists.

- All active sites on the standard cadence; portfolio review chaired from
  the records by rotating Site TPMs, with the lead as a participant. If the
  system only works when the lead runs it, it does not work yet.
- First cross-site dependency and long-pole review: equipment, utility, and
  commissioning-capacity contention across sites made explicit, with float
  trades decided in the portfolio forum and logged.
- Feed delivery-risk input into the next site-selection / due-diligence
  cycle (charter interface), so the function's knowledge starts shaping
  commitments before they are made, not only reporting on them after.
- Retro on the first 90 days with the team and the interface partners:
  which records earned their keep, which reporting was theatre, what gets
  deleted. Deleting a report is a success metric.
- Deliverable at day 90: a functioning review system the team runs, a
  hiring pipeline at the calibrated bar, and a written next-two-quarters
  plan (sites, hires, and the top three portfolio risks to delivery
  capacity).

## What success and failure look like at day 90

- Success: leadership asks the records, not the lead, how a site is doing -
  and the answer survives verification.
- Failure to name honestly: a bigger team producing prettier status with
  the same decision latency. Every element above is ordered to avoid
  exactly that outcome, and the day-90 retro is where it gets judged.
